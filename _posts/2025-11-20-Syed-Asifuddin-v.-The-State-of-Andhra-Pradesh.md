# Syed Asifuddin v. The State of Andhra Pradesh 2006 (1) ALD Cri 96, 2005 CriLJ 4314, (decided on 29 July, 2005 (AP High Court))

## Facts: 
The complaint was lodged by Reliance Infocomm Ltd., alleging that rival service providers were using unethical practices to "woo" their subscribers. The alleged modus operandi involved:


• Inducing Reliance subscribers to shift to other networks while retaining their high-end digital handsets.

• Taking the handsets to undisclosed locations to perform a conversion process.

• Hacking the Electronic Serial Number (ESN) of the Reliance instrument by reprogramming it to function on the TATA Indicom network.

• The investigation revealed that handsets exclusively manufactured for Reliance (Samsung N191 and LG-2030) were being migrated to the TATA network by tampering with pre-programmed digital settings.

## Arguments of the Parties

### Petitioners (TATA Employees):

• Subscribers have the right to change service providers.

• CDMA handsets can accommodate two lines; activating a new line (NAM 1 or NAM 2) is not a crime.

• IPC Offences: No "dishonest appropriation" occurred, so Section 409 (Criminal Breach of Trust) does not apply. No deception or delivery of property occurred, so Section 420 (Cheating) is inapplicable.

• Technical Argument: A cell phone is not a computer or a computer system. Furthermore, there is no law requiring the maintenance of a "computer source code," meaning Section 65 of the IT Act cannot be invoked.

### Respondents (State and Reliance):

• The handsets are dedicated to Reliance’s network; interfering with the internal programming is a technical offence.

• A crime should not be quashed at the investigation stage when highly technical evidence is involved.
