# Syed Asifuddin v. The State of Andhra Pradesh 2006 (1) ALD Cri 96, 2005 CriLJ 4314, (decided on 29 July, 2005 (AP High Court))

## Facts: 
The complaint was lodged by Reliance Infocomm Ltd., alleging that rival service providers were using unethical practices to "woo" their subscribers. The alleged modus operandi involved:

• Inducing Reliance subscribers to shift to other networks while retaining their high-end digital handsets.

• Taking the handsets to undisclosed locations to perform a conversion process.

• Hacking the Electronic Serial Number (ESN) of the Reliance instrument by reprogramming it to function on the TATA Indicom network.

• The investigation revealed that handsets exclusively manufactured for Reliance (Samsung N191 and LG-2030) were being migrated to the TATA network by tampering with pre-programmed digital settings.

## Arguments of the Parties

### Petitioners (TATA Employees):

• Subscribers have the right to change service providers.

• CDMA handsets can accommodate two lines; activating a new line (NAM 1 or NAM 2) is not a crime.

• IPC Offences: No "dishonest appropriation" occurred, so Section 409 (Criminal Breach of Trust) does not apply. No deception or delivery of property occurred, so Section 420 (Cheating) is inapplicable.

• Technical Argument: A cell phone is not a computer or a computer system. Furthermore, there is no law requiring the maintenance of a "computer source code," meaning Section 65 of the IT Act cannot be invoked.

### Respondents (State and Reliance):

• The handsets are dedicated to Reliance’s network; interfering with the internal programming is a technical offence.

• A crime should not be quashed at the investigation stage when highly technical evidence is involved.

Whoever, being in any manner entrusted with property, or with any dominion over property in his capacity of a public servant or in the way of his business as a banker, merchant, factor, broker, attorney or agent, commits criminal breach of trust in respect of that property, shall be punished with imprisonment for life, or with imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to ten years, and shall also be liable to fine.

**IPC Section 420.   Cheating and dishonestly inducing delivery of property.** 

Whoever cheats and thereby dishonestly induces the person deceived to deliver any property to any person, or to make, alter or destroy the whole or any part of a valuable security, or anything which is signed or sealed, and which is capable of being converted into a valuable security, shall be punished with imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to seven years, and shall also be liable to fine.

## Observations of the Court:

### Regarding Criminal Breach of Trust (S.409 IPC (Now S.316(5) BNS))

For this crime, there must be entrustment of property which is missing in this case as the property is still with the subscriber and not with TATA. Essential ingredient of CBT missing. 

### Regarding Cheating (S.420 IPC (Now S.318(4) BNS)

Absence of fraudulent inducement to deliver the property. Thus essential ingredient of Cheating missing too. 

### Reliance on past precedents to stop police proceedings wrt S.409 and S.420 IPC

Relying on State of West Bengal v. Swapan Kumar and State of Haryana v. Bhajan Lal, the court said that the police does not have power to investigate a case in which the FIR does not disclose the commission of cognizable offence. Here the FIR failed to meet the essential ingredients of the above mentioned sections and to allow the police proceedings wrt these offences would lead to miscarriage of justice. 

### Regarding the question of whether a cell phone would be included in the definition of computer u/s 2(1)(i) IT Act 2000 or not

The court ruled that a cell phone would be covered by the definition of computer u/s 2(1)(i) of IT Act 2000 as it is a device that is capable of performing logical, arithmetic and memory functions and the circuit board in it processes digital and analog signals, much like the CPU of a desktop/computer.

### Regarding the categorisation of ESN and SID as a "computer source code" (CSC) u/s 65 of IT Act 2000

The court determined that they would be covered under the definition of CSC because they (electronic serial number and system identification code) are programmed commands that allow a device to communicate to specific network. 

### Regarding the contention that for an act to constitute an offence u/s 65 of the IT Act, it must involve a source code that is required to be maintained by law

The court, rejecting this technical defence, observed that there is a disjunctive word "or" between "source is required to be kept" and " maintained by law" which meant that even where there is no legal requirement to maintain the source code, it would still be an offence if the source code in question was one that was simply being kept as was the case in the present case. 

### Regarding the question of copyright infringement because of alleged alteration of the code developed by Reliance.

The court observed that the Copyright Act 1957 was amended in 1995 to include computer programs as literary work ( see s.2(o),2(ffb) and 2(ffc) of Copyright Act 1957) and s. 14 of the act gives exclusive rights with respect the work. if there is alteration of the code then prima facie it would constitute infringement of copyright under the act. 

## Decision of the Court
The Court issued a split decision:

1. Quashed the charges under Sections 409, 420, and 120B of the IPC, as the F.I.R. did not disclose these offences.
  
2. Dismissed the petitions regarding Section 65 of the IT Act and Section 63 of the Copyright Act, allowing the investigation into these technical and digital offences to continue
